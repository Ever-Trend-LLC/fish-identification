Florida Fish Identification Chart: 26 Species with Pictures

Florida touches the Gulf of Mexico, the Atlantic and the subtropical Keys, and adds a freshwater fishery that grows the biggest largemouth bass in the country. This Florida fish identification chart pairs a photo of each species you are most likely to catch or see, from the Panhandle to Key West, with the traits that separate it from its look-alikes. Florida fish identification by coast

Panhandle and Gulf Coast

Pensacola to Naples. Cooler, less tropical water: red snapper, gag grouper, king and Spanish mackerel, cobia in spring, redfish and seatrout on the grass flats, sheepshead on every piling.

Atlantic Coast

Jacksonville to Miami. Sailfish, mahi-mahi and wahoo off the Gulf Stream edge; snook and tarpon in the inlets; pompano and whiting in the surf.

Florida Keys

The only living coral barrier reef in the continental US. Bonefish, permit and tarpon on the flats; hogfish, mutton and yellowtail snapper on the patch reefs; goliath grouper on the wrecks.

Freshwater

Lakes, canals and rivers: Florida-strain largemouth bass, peacock bass in the Miami canals, black crappie ("specks") in the big lakes, alligator gar in Panhandle rivers.

Florida fish identification FAQ

What is the most common fish caught in Florida?

Inshore it is spotted seatrout and redfish on both coasts, with snook in the south. Offshore, red snapper and gag grouper dominate the Gulf side and mahi-mahi, sailfish and yellowtail snapper the Atlantic side.

How do I tell a redfish from a black drum?

Feel under the chin: black drum have a row of small barbels, redfish have none. Redfish are copper-bronze with one or more tail spots; black drum are grey-silver with bars in young fish.

Which Florida fish must be released?

Goliath grouper is fully protected, and snook, redfish, tarpon and permit have tight slot limits, seasons and regional closures. Check the FWC rules for your coast before keeping a fish — the identifier tells you the species, not the season.
