Gulf of Mexico Fish Identification Chart: 26 Species with Pictures

The Gulf of Mexico is one of the most productive fisheries on Earth: shallow bays and seagrass flats, a wide shelf of hard bottom, oil platforms that act as reefs, and blue water beyond the shelf edge. This Gulf of Mexico fish identification chart pairs a photo of each species anglers meet from the Texas coast to the Florida panhandle with the traits that separate it from its look-alikes. Gulf of Mexico fish identification by habitat

Bays and grass flats

Galveston, Matagorda, Laguna Madre, Mobile Bay, Apalachicola: speckled trout, redfish, black drum, flounder, sheepshead and croaker. Most Gulf fishing happens here.

Surf, passes and jetties

Spanish mackerel, pompano, whiting, crevalle jack, ladyfish and blacktip sharks in summer; bull redfish and tarpon in autumn.

Reefs and oil platforms (20–100 m)

Red and vermilion snapper, gag grouper, amberjack, cobia and king mackerel. Platforms off Louisiana and Texas hold the densest snapper stocks.

Blue water beyond the shelf

Yellowfin tuna, wahoo, mahi-mahi and blue marlin along the shelf edge and floating structure.

Gulf of Mexico fish identification by state

  • Texas: over 1,000 km of coast and the hypersaline Laguna Madre; the state's big three are speckled trout, redfish and flounder, with tarpon off Port O'Connor and platform snapper offshore.
  • Louisiana: the marsh delta produces more redfish and trout than any other state; offshore platforms hold red snapper, amberjack and yellowfin tuna within a short run.
  • Mississippi and Alabama: Mobile Bay and the barrier islands for trout, redfish and tripletail on the crab-trap buoys; Alabama's artificial reef zone is the largest in the US for red snapper.
  • Florida Panhandle: Pensacola, Destin and Panama City: cobia run in spring, king mackerel in summer, gag grouper on nearshore ledges, and clear water for sight-casting.

Gulf of Mexico fish identification FAQ

What is the most common fish in the Gulf of Mexico?

Inshore, spotted seatrout and redfish are the most caught species in every Gulf state from Texas to Florida. Offshore, red snapper is the signature Gulf fish, with vermilion snapper, gag grouper and amberjack on the same reefs and platforms.

How do I tell speckled trout from a weakfish or sand seatrout?

Speckled (spotted) seatrout have distinct round black spots on the back, dorsal and tail. Sand seatrout and silver seatrout are plain silver-grey with no spots; weakfish have faint diagonal streaks and are rare in the Gulf.

Are there sharks near Gulf beaches?

Yes: blacktip, spinner, bull and bonnethead sharks work the surf and passes in summer. Blacktips are the ones most often seen jumping and caught from the beach; the black fin tips and pointed snout identify them.
