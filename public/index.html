How the fish identifier works

  1. Take or upload the photoSnap the fish with your phone camera or pick a picture from your library. Your browser shrinks it before sending, so even large phone photos upload quickly. Nothing is published.
  2. The AI looks for a fishThe image is checked for a fish and for photo quality. If no fish is visible you are told straight away, so you can retake the shot.
  3. You get the speciesThe top match comes back with its common name, scientific name, a confidence rating and care tips, with up to two alternatives. Save it to your tank in the FishCare AI app with one tap.

The whole round trip normally takes two to five seconds. If nothing is detected, the answer to “what fish is this” is usually hiding behind a photo problem rather than a rare species — the next section covers that.

How to photograph a fish so the ID is accurate

When people fail to identify fish from a photo, the photo is nearly always the reason rather than the species being rare. These six things account for most failed matches:

  • Shoot the whole fish from the side. A lateral profile shows body depth, fin placement and stripe pattern — the three features the model leans on hardest. Head-on and top-down shots are the most common cause of a wrong answer.
  • Keep the dorsal and tail fins in frame. Fin shape separates species that otherwise look identical. A cropped tail throws away that signal.
  • Fill at least a third of the frame. A fish that is 40 pixels wide in a wide tank shot carries almost no detail to classify.
  • Kill the glare. Turn off the flash, shoot straight at the glass rather than at an angle, and avoid heavy blue or actinic lighting — colour casts shift the match badly on reef fish.
  • One fish per photo where you can. Multiple fish work, but a single clear subject gives the cleanest answer.
  • For a caught fish, keep it wet and use a plain background. Dry, faded colour and a busy background of grass or gravel both reduce accuracy.

Reading the confidence rating: a High match is usually reliable. Moderate means a shortlist — confirm against the species guide. Limited means the model is guessing — retake the photo from the side before you trust the name.

If you have no photo to hand, or you want to sanity-check what the AI returned, body shape narrows it down fast. Start with the outline, then the fins, then the colour — colour is the least reliable of the three because it changes with age, diet, lighting and breeding condition.

What this fish identification tool covers

Recognition runs on Google’s Gemini vision model through the FishCare AI service. It looks at body shape, fin placement, colour pattern and markings and returns up to three candidate species, most likely first, each with a High, Moderate or Limited confidence rating. When the top match is in the FishCare AI species library you can save it to your tank in the app with one tap.

Coverage spans the common freshwater aquarium trade, marine reef fish, pond fish such as koi and goldfish, and wild fish you have caught. For the top match you get the common and scientific name and a short list of care tips; the full care card lives in the app. Photos are sent to Google Gemini only to identify the fish — they are not used for model training and are not stored long term (see the privacy policy).

Where it struggles

  • Selectively bred aquarium morphs. Fancy guppies, flowerhorn, blood parrot cichlids and heavily line-bred bettas often look nothing like the wild species the model learned.
  • Juveniles. Many species carry completely different markings before maturity.
  • Hybrids and fish that are damaged, faded or partly obscured.

Not for regulatory use. Do not rely on this tool to decide whether a catch is legal to keep. Size and season rules hinge on exact species, and a confident-looking match is not a substitute for a warden, an official ID key or a local regulations guide.

Frequently asked questions

Can I really find out what fish this is from just a photo?

Yes. A clear side-on photo is normally enough to get a species-level answer, and ordinary phone pictures of aquarium fish, pond fish and freshly caught fish work well.

Is this fish identifier free?

Yes. Your first identifications are free with no sign-up or app download. A free FishCare AI account adds more every month (shared with the FishCare AI app), and Plus removes the limit. The page shows how many you have left.

How accurate is AI fish identification?

Each result comes with up to three candidate species and a High, Moderate or Limited confidence rating. Accuracy depends heavily on photo quality, angle and lighting: treat High as a reliable answer, Moderate as a shortlist to check against a care guide, and Limited as a guess. Retake the photo side-on before relying on a Limited match.

What kind of fish is this — freshwater or saltwater?

The result tells you. The top match comes with its scientific name and care tips that say what water it needs, and the identifier covers freshwater, marine and pond fish, so you do not need to know which you have before uploading.

Why does it say no fish detected?

Almost always a photo issue rather than a missing species. The usual causes are the fish being too small in the frame, heavy glass glare, motion blur, or a head-on angle. Retake the shot from the side with the fish filling more of the frame.

Can it identify more than one fish in the same photo?

Crop to one fish for the best answer. The identifier names the most prominent fish in the photo; for a mixed tank, take one photo per species.

Does it work on screenshots and photos taken through glass?

Both work. Screenshots from video are fine if the frame is sharp. Shooting through aquarium glass is fine too as long as you shoot square to the glass rather than at an angle, and switch the flash off.

What happens to the photo I upload?

It is sent to the recognition service to be analysed and the result is returned to your browser. It is not published on the site or added to a public gallery.
